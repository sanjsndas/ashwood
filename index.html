Frequently asked questions

Do I really need to dig my soil?

No — in fact, digging can damage the delicate fungal networks and soil structure. No-dig gardening builds fertility by layering organic matter on top, letting worms and microbes do the work.

What is the best time to start a vegetable garden?

It depends on your climate, but early spring or autumn are ideal. Autumn planting lets roots establish before winter, while spring gives warmth-loving crops a head start.

How do I improve clay soil without chemicals?

Add plenty of organic matter — compost, leaf mould, well-rotted manure — and avoid walking on beds when wet. Over time, clay becomes a rich, workable loam.

Can I garden in small spaces?

Absolutely. Containers, vertical planters, and windowsill herbs can produce a surprising amount. Focus on high-value crops like salad leaves, tomatoes, and herbs.

About Ashwood Botanical Tales

Independent · Ad-free · Reader-supported

Ashwood Botanical Tales began in 2016 as a personal garden diary — a place to record what worked, what failed, and the quiet lessons that only come from hands in the soil. Today, it's a small independent publication read by over 40,000 gardeners each month.

We believe gardening is a practical art and a slow conversation with nature. Our articles are written by experienced growers, tested in real gardens, and free from corporate influence. We don't sell plants, pots, or miracle cures. We share what we've learned.

Our editorial focus is simple: deep, useful, seasonal. You'll find no listicles of "10 miracle plants" — instead, long-form guides on soil health, propagation, design, and the rhythms of the growing year.

Elena Marsh — Editor & founder. Elena has been growing food and flowers for over 20 years, and trained in organic horticulture at the Royal Botanic Gardens.

Marcus Reed — Contributing editor. Marcus specialises in soil biology and no-dig systems, and runs a small market garden in the Cotswolds.

We're proudly reader-supported. If you value what we do, consider joining our newsletter — it's free, and it helps us keep the site independent.
